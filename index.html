Electrical Safety Enclosures — Stirling Engine System

Results
100+Parts in structured BOMs
40+Unique custom parts

Enclosure fabricated and integrated into the pilot engine system. Improved electrical safety margin and service access for ongoing testing.

Objective
Design an electrical safety enclosure for integration within a high-temperature Stirling engine environment. Key requirements: passive thermal ventilation, serviceability, and vibration-resistant structural mounting.
How
Modeled in Fusion 360 with passive venting channels for thermal management. Developed vibration-resistant mounting hardware and prepared drawings for CNC fabrication. Performed coupled thermal-structural FEA to evaluate component performance under operating conditions.
Design and drawings
I design mechanical components and assemblies in Fusion 360 for a pilot-scale thermal energy system, including a sheet metal resistor enclosure with environmental sealing. I produce drawing packages to ASME Y14.5 and take parts from requirements through prototype builds and component fabrication.
PLM and BOMs
I created an in-house PLM process using the built-in properties of Fusion 360 for BOM structuring, revision control, engineering changes, and nonconformance reports (NCRs). I structured BOMs of 100+ parts, including 40+ unique custom parts.
Analysis and testing
I run structural and coupled thermal-structural finite element analysis (FEA) to guide design changes. I also set up and ran engine heat transfer tests and analyzed the data.
Teamwork and IP
I work within a five-person team, partnering with electrical and manufacturing teammates on design for manufacturing (DFM) and design for assembly (DFA) so parts get built, assembled, and installed. I built the intellectual property (IP) commercialization plan and presented the technical decisions, analysis results, and design tradeoffs that informed pilot deployment.
